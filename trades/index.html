The problem

A customer texts you while you're on a job. The name sits in a thread. The follow-up slips. The work order is on a pad in the truck. By the time you get back to it, they already called someone else.

The booking page

You get a simple page in your name, with your prices, your towns, and your phone number.

  • They see the price first, when you have a set price.
  • They pick a town, a service, and a day that works.
  • It opens a text to you with their name, town, service, price, and preferred day already filled in.
  • They hit send in their own Messages app. You can also put a call button on the page.

You can put the link in your bio, on a flyer, or in a post. Here is the one I use: Bee's Service and AC booking page.

The CRM, on your phone

Add it to your iPhone or Android Home Screen. No app store.

  • A Today screen: who to call, who's late, what's next.
  • Leads, follow-ups, and a pipeline you can see as a board or a calendar.
  • Ready texts: tap one, it opens in your own Messages app with the words filled in. You read it and hit send. Nothing goes out on its own. Campaign texts include Reply STOP to opt out.
  • Forms: service agreements, work orders, estimates, and invoices. The customer signs with a finger. You get a PDF you can text or email.
  • It works with no signal. Leads live on the phone first. Forms wait and go out when you're back in range.
  • Your data stays yours. Back it up to a Google Sheet in your name. Export to CSV anytime.
  • Your look: business name, logo, colors, and phone. You can send a setup link so a tech's phone matches the shop.

What this is not

This is not a big-shop dispatch system. It does not take customer card payments in the app, plan routes, track GPS, run payroll, or sync to QuickBooks. It does not send texts for you. You stay in control of every message.
